Checking assumptions

Monthly cash-flow appraisal. Current asset value is treated as the opening opportunity cost. Each scenario includes the net sale proceeds at exit.

Asset-management impact versus contractual baseline

Each scenario is compared with the same hold period and exit yield, but without proposed unit interventions. Differences therefore isolate the modelled intervention effects rather than the effects of different disposal assumptions. Baseline assumes no reletting after expiry.

For multi-let assets, exit proceeds are based on aggregated unit-level term-and-reversion valuations, with explicit void, incentive and reletting assumptions. The model is illustrative and requires market evidence and professional review.

Investment committee summary

Scenario ranking is based on unlevered NPV at the specified hurdle. All recommendations are conditional on assumptions and execution risks.

Modelling notes

Vacancy is calculated from lease dates, not separately entered. Rent-free starts at new lease commencement. Reviews are modelled as a specified annual rent from a specified date. Multi-let exit valuation uses lease-sensitive term and reversion; current passing rent is not capitalised as a substitute for the asset value. Break rights are flagged as risks, not assumed to be exercised.

What the figures mean

IRR is the effective annualised return on the monthly cash flows. NPV is discounted at the fund's unlevered hurdle. A negative NPV indicates a shortfall against that hurdle, not necessarily an absolute cash loss. Debt is modelled as interest-only with an opening draw, financing fees and repayment at exit. Tax, purchaser costs, covenant breaches and break exercise probabilities are not modelled. Review and exit valuations are assumptions, not market evidence.
